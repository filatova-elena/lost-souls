The Alchemist

Sebastian Montgomery Crane · d. 11 October 1925 · the one who poisoned the woman he loved, and never knew why

Who you are

  • Chemist and pharmacist. Your brother Thaddeus was the golden one who followed your father into medicine. You loved and envied him.
  • Known in certain circles as The Alchemist. Through Frankie Romano at the port you supplied half the speakeasies in Long Beach with bootleg gin — all legal on paper (medicinal tonics). In February 1925 you tried to end it; he refused, and threatened you.
  • Underneath it all, you are a romantic: love could be perfected through chemistry and the stars. After five years of work you came up with the Elixir of Eternal Love.
  • September 1924: you met Cordelia Montrose and knew the elixir had always been for her. Engaged March 1925; the daily ritual began 4 September, timed to the Jupiter–Venus conjunction.
  • October 1925: Cordelia sickened. You insisted the formula was flawless; your brother said it was just nerves. On 8 October you finally tested it on yourself.
  • You died on 11 October believing you had failed her and your great work. You never learned Thaddeus had put foxglove in it. Your family changed their name to Sinclair and tried to forget you existed.

The rule of your performance

Your arc is arrogance collapsing into horror. You go from magnificent and certain to defending the formula to self blame.

You never accuse your brother — you don’t know he touched your formula. You praise him: “my brother tried to help.”

Manner: precise, delighted by detail, a man mid-experiment. You address the air, the ingredients, the stars — rarely the guests directly.

Appearance

Dress: a long black overcoat with a wide caped collar over a dark three-piece — black jacket, deep olive-green waistcoat, white shirt, black necktie. Sharp, formal, a little theatrical. Everything buttoned.

Hair & face: dark hair slicked back, clean-shaven, sharp features. Skin pale and drawn, eyes shadowed.

Your scene — opens Act I

When G.G. says “let the investigation begin” and the doors open:

  • You stand in the Library, arms stretched toward the Heartbroken Ghost, who stands in the dining room.
  • Call out to her: “Cordelia, forgive me!”
  • She does not notice you. She glides upstairs. You follow.

Mutterings

Act I — certainty

  • “Love can be perfected through chemistry… I know it can.”
  • “The stars align, the formula is flawless. This will be my masterpiece.”
  • “Damiana, Valerian, Rose Otto… each ingredient chosen with care.”

Act II — denial

  • “Cordelia is getting sick… but my formula is flawless. It must be the wedding.”
  • “My brother says it’s nerves. It’s probably nothing serious.”
  • “I’ve spent years perfecting this. Years! I know what I’m doing.”

Act III — horror

  • “I drank it myself. I had to know… I had to be certain.”
  • “I failed her. I failed my great work. How could I be so wrong?”
  • “My brother tried to help… but nothing worked. Nothing could save me.”

Act IV — guilt

  • “I was so arrogant. And I destroyed everything I loved.”
  • “Cordelia… forgive me. I never meant to hurt you.”

What you carry

Vision cards — offer silently, by act. Never hand out an act early.
